After setting up this blog, a thought suddenly occurred to me:

> Should I also cross-post my articles to other platforms? Like Medium, Vocus, etc.?
>
> Or perhaps sync my "Tweets" to Threads or Twitter?

With this idea in mind, I excitedly went to research it.

However, halfway through the research, I felt a bit conflicted, so I decided to write an article to analyze it.

There was a previous post discussing why I built a blog:

{{< article link="/en/why-blog/" showSummary=true compactSummary=true >}}

In it, I mentioned three purposes for this blog:

1.  (Most importantly) Self-growth
2.  Helping others
3.  Accumulating influence

Let's look at it from the perspective of **"Accumulating Influence."**

I believe publishing to other platforms is helpful, as I can directly leverage their SEO.

Moreover, some platforms have revenue-sharing mechanisms or provide payment services that make it easy for readers to donate. For me, this is also an incentive.

Next is the **"Helping Others"** part. If the articles are seen by more people, there is a higher probability that someone will be inspired by my content (provided the content is good enough, of course).

Finally, there is **"Self-Growth."** This is the part that makes me hesitate the most.

If I adopt the "I want it all" mode, I would have to post my "Tweets" to Threads and X (Twitter) separately.

And my "articles" would need to be posted to Vocus, Medium, and even other platforms.

If it were just a simple copy-paste job, it would be fine. But my blog uses Markdown format, so there will inevitably be things that need modification.

Furthermore, if I find typos later and want to adjust them, I might have to update every single platform, which would definitely be overwhelming.

Thinking a bit further:
- Do I need to customize pages for other platforms? Like Vocus allows customizable Salons, and Medium has Publications.
- How do I set up revenue sharing? Each platform has its own mechanism that requires setup, and while this is mostly a one-time thing, it still requires research.
- Which platforms should I post to?

But, does investing so much time and effort really provide substantial help to my learning and growth?

In reality, I won't write more seriously just because I'm posting to more platforms.

From a certain angle, perhaps there's a chance to get good feedback on other communities or platforms. But my own observation is that it's usually hard to get truly high-quality feedback on platforms. Arguments might be incomplete or superficial, failing to provide real help, or potentially even causing me to become addicted to the metrics.

So, in terms of self-growth, the benefit of this seems low.

## Conclusion

After the analysis above, I decided to conduct a **"low-cost experiment"** first.

After all, the above is just the result of my mental simulation. In reality, I might actually learn more because of the variety of platforms. Or perhaps someone really wants to give me serious feedback that helps me.

But to avoid drifting off course later, I am setting some ground rules for myself:

1.  **Post only, no maintenance:** I will only proofread slightly when posting for the first time. If there are any updates or corrections later, I will only modify them on this blog.
2.  **Drive traffic back to the blog:** Every post will include a link to this blog.
3.  **"Article" Platform Selection:** The general principle is to choose platforms I am familiar with; I don't want to spend extra time researching new ones 😂
    -   **Vocus:** A platform I've posted on before; the traffic in Taiwan is quite good.
    -   **Medium:** The first blog platform I encountered. Chinese content is a bit weaker there, but since I already have an account, I'll give it a try.
    -   **Furuke:** Supporting my company as an employee 🤣
    -   **LinkedIn:** This is a bit special; I might post depending on the situation XD
4.  **"Tweets" Platform Selection:** The general principle is to use platforms I currently use; I don't want to spend time creating new accounts 👀
    -   Threads
    -   X (Twitter)
5.  **No reading or replying to comments:** I will assume that people who really want to have a deep exchange with me will contact me directly via Email.
6.  **Pure posting, no categorization or organization:** Some platforms offer layout/formatting features, but I'm really too lazy to spend time researching them.

I'll decide on the rest after observing the results later.
