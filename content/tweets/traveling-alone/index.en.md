In recent years, on social media in Taiwan, I often see people emphasizing where they went for "solo travel".

In self-introductions, some people even specifically separate "solo travel" from "non-solo travel," listing which countries they visited alone and which they visited with others.

There are even groups of friends who go abroad together, agree to have a free afternoon, and count that afternoon as "solo travel".

This situation puzzles me. For me, travel is travel. Whether it's sharing joy with a group or enjoying freedom alone, I enjoy it all. I've never thought about making a special distinction for travel.

If it's for content creators, I can understand, as some people might need to find shops suitable for one person.

But for ordinary people, isn't it a bit unnecessary?

I couldn't help but wonder, what is the charm of the term "solo travel"? 🤔

So I asked Gemini about this question, and here is an excerpt:

> Simply put, the charm of the term "solo travel" has gone beyond the act of "traveling alone" itself; it has been endowed with more meanings regarding personal growth, independence, and attitude towards life.
>
> We can understand its charm from the following aspects:
>
> 1. A Symbol of Self-Exploration and Growth
>
> In modern society with its fast pace and high pressure, we rarely have time to truly be alone and talk to ourselves. "Solo travel" creates a perfect opportunity, forcing us to break away from familiar social circles and comfort zones.
>
> - **Ability to solve problems independently:** From planning the itinerary and booking accommodation to dealing with unexpected situations, everything must be done independently. Every successful problem-solving experience accumulates into a huge sense of accomplishment and self-confidence, which is an experience difficult to obtain in group travel.
>
> - **Deep introspection:** When there are no travel companions to chat and distract you, you will have more time to think. What you see and hear on the journey is more likely to trigger reflection on your life and values. Many people feel that their future direction is clearer after solo travel.
> 
> 2. The Ultimate Experience of "Complete Freedom"
> 
> Group travel, even with close friends, inevitably requires coordination and compromise. Who wants to go to the museum today, what to eat for lunch, how long to shop... these are all sweet burdens.
>
> - **Freedom without compromise:** Solo travel means 100% autonomy. You can sleep until you wake up naturally, or get up at 4 am to shoot the sunrise. You can spend the whole afternoon reading in a cafe, or jump on a train to an unheard-of small town on a whim. This feeling of "living completely for yourself" is a luxury in itself.
>
> - **Courage to embrace uncertainty:** Traveling alone makes it easier to encounter unexpected people and things. Because you are alone, your mindset will be more open, and you will be more willing to communicate with locals and try new things. This unknown and surprise is one of the most fascinating parts of solo travel.
>
> 3. "Identity Labels" on Social Media
> 
> This also explains why everyone on social media emphasizes "solo travel". In the era of personal branding, what we do shapes how others see us.
>
> - **Image of bravery and independence:** Saying that you are "solo traveling" or "like solo traveling" is like telling others: "I am an independent, opinionated person who is not afraid of being alone and dares to challenge." This is a very positive social label.
>
> - **Finding a sense of belonging in the community:** Through tags like #SoloTravel, you can find like-minded people with similar experiences and values, forming a sense of belonging in an online community. Sharing solo travel tips and experiences is also a social behavior in itself.

---

I'm not sure if others find this analysis reasonable. I feel that the first two points are more like analyzing the differences of solo travel, while I think the third point, "Identity Labels," answers my doubts.

However, if that's really the case, I find it a bit of a pity.

There is a saying in *The Analects*:

> At first, my way with men was to hear their words and give them credit for their conduct. Now my way is to hear their words and look at their conduct.

The general meaning is: In the beginning, when dealing with people, I would listen to what they said and trust their actions; now, when dealing with people, I listen to what they said and also observe their actual actions.

For example, someone might frantically praise how good cryptocurrency is and keep telling others to invest, but they haven't bought a single coin themselves. This is a typical case of "saying no with their mouth, but their body is honest" (inconsistency).

Similarly, if someone says they can enjoy solitude but needs to deliberately put a label on themselves on social media... can that still be considered enjoying solitude?

I feel that this "I am independent, but I want you to know I am independent" actually reflects some kind of insecurity deep inside.

True strength is usually quiet and doesn't need to be proven through broadcasting.

Deliberately labeling it as "solo travel" sometimes seems like a defense mechanism: "Look, I'm doing very well on my own!"

It's like someone constantly emphasizing that they "don't care about other people's opinions," but the very act of emphasizing it usually means they actually care a lot.
