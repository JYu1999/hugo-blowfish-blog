I went for a health checkup today, and a couple of interesting things happened. I thought I'd share them.

## Slow Heart Rate

When the nurse was measuring my blood pressure, she suddenly said to me: "Hey, your heart rate is a bit slow."

I was stunned and thought, "Huh? What?" No one had ever told me this before.

Later, during another examination, another nurse said exactly the same thing.

I asked the nurse why this was happening, and she said it might be because of exercise.

But I was thinking, my exercise volume doesn't seem to be that high. Although compared to the past few years, my exercise volume has indeed increased a lot this year, when compared to when I was a Muay Thai fighter, the exercise volume still seems quite different.

I don't know why this was suddenly discovered this year, because no one had ever mentioned it before.

The nurse said that if my heart rate is slow, I might feel dizzy or something, but I feel fine so far, so there should be no problem with this.

## Veins Too Thin

The second finding was during the blood draw.

I've noticed that after I grew up, many nurses have told me that my veins are difficult to draw from because they're too thin.

When I was a child, no one ever told me this. It wasn't until after I started working that people began telling me. I find it a bit strange—why did no one mention this when I was young?

Today was the most painful blood draw experience of my life.

The nurse first tried my left arm and said: "Your veins are too thin." After the needle went in, it seemed like the vein got flattened or ruptured—I'm not sure—but anyway, after drawing for a bit, she couldn't get any blood.

Then she kept moving the needle back and forth inside, trying to find the vein. That process was really agonizing, and eventually she gave up.

Then she tried my right arm. She also thought the veins were too thin and inserted the needle. At that moment, whether compared to previous injections or blood draws, I had never felt such pain.

And she was also moving the needle around inside, especially when changing the blood collection tubes—it was really painful. Every time she changed the tube and the needle moved, my skin would hurt terribly.

Then she asked me: "Are you feeling faint?" Because I was holding my head, just wanting it to end quickly.

I told her: "No, it just hurts a lot."

She said: "If you feel faint, you need to tell me, because many people don't mention it when they feel faint, and then they end up passing out."

I've never had such a painful blood draw experience in my entire life XD
