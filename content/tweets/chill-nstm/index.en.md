Today, I went to the Science and Technology Museum with my partner to see an exhibition. When we came out, we found many elders doing arm-swinging exercises, someone playing the erhu, and the sound of children laughing and playing.

The weather was cool and very comfortable.

Actually, in the past, I used to spend most of my weekends cooped up at home, forcing myself to keep learning new things.

If I accidentally slacked off for a day, I would feel extremely guilty, wondering how I could be so lazy.

But a series of events this year made me realize that my life isn't really about pursuing wealth, fame, or achievement. Instead, it's about pursuing inner peace.

For me, peace means **immersing myself in the present moment**. When drinking tea, simply feeling its temperature and aroma; when walking, focusing on every step under my feet.

So now, on weekends, I don't schedule anything. If I want to go out, I go out; if I want to stay home and do chores, I do that; if I want to study, that's fine too. I just let myself relax and immerse myself in whatever I want to do in the moment.

I feel that this adjustment, firstly, gives me breathing room, so I don't feel guilty for not working hard.

Secondly, it allows me to better distinguish between work and life. Otherwise, I used to have the feeling of "I can do it on the weekend anyway", which actually led to a lack of focus.

I recommend everyone take a walk at the Science and Technology Museum if you have the chance~
