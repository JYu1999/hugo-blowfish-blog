A few days ago, I messaged my Vietnamese teacher to ask when she'd be in Japan this year — maybe we could meet up.

She replied with a Vietnamese voice message and a Chinese voice message.

It reminded me that she's already an impressive person — as far as I know, she speaks four languages: Vietnamese (her native tongue), English, Japanese, and Chinese. I've always admired her, and her teaching is excellent.

What struck me this time was how naturally she seizes every opportunity to practice.

I've been thinking — speaking practice is a pain point for a lot of people. We often feel like there's "no environment" to practice in, but the truth is, you can create one yourself.

So when I replied to her, I switched entirely to Japanese — since that's what I'm currently studying.

The process was a bit makeshift: I typed what I wanted to say, ran it through ChatGPT for translation, then fed the translation into ElevenLabs for Text-to-Speech. I listened repeatedly, shadowed the audio until it felt natural, then recorded myself and sent it back.

It was actually a really interesting process — it forces you to think about how to express your own thoughts, rather than just repeating sentences from a textbook or YouTube video.

Pure input only teaches you how others speak. You have to start outputting to learn how to say things your own way.
