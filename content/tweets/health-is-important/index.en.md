[OnlyFans owner Leonid Radvinsky dies of cancer at 43! Built an "adult subscription empire" with a net worth over $4 billion](https://www.blocktempo.com/onlyfans-owner-leonid-radvinsky-dies-at-43-cancer/)

I just saw this news and felt inspired to share some thoughts.

Recently, I've seen quite a few people I admire experiencing health issues, which naturally gives me a heavy heart.

Everyone says "health is important", but I've observed that many people, even when their bodies are clearly showing signs of trouble, still brush it off and keep pushing forward.

Their reason is often: "I really can't let go."

It's a bit like what Gipi mentioned in his article [#8 The Gift I Received After Two Years of Illness](https://gipi.tw/gipi-newsletter-v8/):

> Before I got sick, I would emphasize life—life should be comfortable, have spare capacity, and have freedom of choice. But my life was actually overshadowed by the subconscious thought "I am starting a business, and starting a business is hard." So I couldn't live well at all. I had financial leeway and time leeway. I once thought my psychological leeway was sufficient as well, but clearly, I overestimated myself.
> 
> My mind could bear the pressure, but the problem reflected physically. This meant that what I called stress relief or decompression didn't actually work; I was just persuading myself, but hadn't solved the problem.

I don't want to point fingers at other people's values, but seeing so many brutal examples—and experiencing some physical and mental health issues myself last year—made me realize even more: "Most of the time, you have to walk slowly to walk far."

Work will inevitably be busy at times, and life will always throw some curveballs. This is all very normal. But I think it's absolutely necessary to properly schedule some breathing room for yourself.

Another quote from Gipi also moved me:

> But the thing causing my persistent stress was actually very clear: my lack of honesty. Being dishonest with myself and dishonest with others frequently became the factor draining my inner energy.
> 
> Only by pursuing inner peace can you reduce internal friction and truly relieve stress. When I realized this, I started practicing ways to lower that internal friction.

I think what Gipi is saying matches the "pure person" I mentioned in [this article](/tweets/pure-person/), except my article only mentioned "being honest with others," and not "being honest with oneself."
