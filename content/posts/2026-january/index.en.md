### Shifts at Work

After discovering this, I started thinking: Is it possible for me to help the team become more efficient in my current job?

I tried analyzing what the company might currently need, and I found I could roughly cut in from three angles:

1.  **Team Collaboration**: Including how to reduce friction, improve consensus, how to motivate employees, etc.
2.  **Development Efficiency**: How to reduce repetitive work, increase development speed, and increase the productivity of other departments.
3.  **Server Management**: How to make our servers more stable, safer, and make debugging more systematic and efficient.

Some people might think: You're just a backend engineer, isn't thinking about these things overstepping? None of this is your business, right?

But then I thought of a concept mentioned in *Atomic Habits*: Identity-based habits.

The book argues that the most effective way to change behavior is not to focus on "what you want to achieve," but to focus on "what kind of person you want to become."

The author compares habit change to an onion, divided into three layers from the outside in:

1.  **Outcomes**: Your goals. For example: I want to lose 5 kg, I want to finish writing a book.
2.  **Processes**: Your systems. For example: Go to the gym every day, write 500 words every day.
3.  **Identity**: Your beliefs. For example: I am an athlete, I am a writer.

Most people start from "outcomes" and push inwards, but *Atomic Habits* suggests we should start from the core (identity) and push outwards.

Why is this effective?

Because when your behavior aligns with your "identity," you are no longer "trying hard" to maintain a habit, but are "demonstrating" your essence. For example:

-   **Typical person**: "No thanks, I'm quitting smoking." (This implies they still feel like a smoker deep down, just trying to resist temptation.)
-   **Person with identity**: "No thanks, I don't smoke." (This implies smoking is no longer part of their self-image, so refusal becomes natural.)

The reason this method is powerful is that it solves the problem of "willpower depletion." When you believe from the bottom of your heart that you are a certain type of person, doing those habits is no longer a burden, but a natural expression.

And I feel I want to apply this concept to my work too.

I want to improve team collaboration? Then I am an Engineering Manager.

I want to improve development efficiency? Then I am an AI Engineer.

I want to manage servers well? Then I am an SRE Leader.

### Constantly Proving Value is Really Tiring

I mentioned above that I discovered the importance of proving value.

But I find this thing quite tiring. Value is actually somewhat difficult to quantify, and value doesn't necessarily translate into rewards.

I was actually thinking, would I be suited for a job where value delivery is more direct?

For example, stock investing. If the strategy is wrong, you lose money. If the strategy is right, you make money. The "value" of the strategy can be directly quantified and reflected in the returns.

Just this month I saw a video:

{{< youtubeLite id="dV_FjwwQfiE" label="Creator x Entrepreneur: From breakdown and quitting to earning 700k/year with side hustles | Ami Interview" >}}

It suddenly made me wonder, should I start a side hustle?

There are mainly a few considerations:

1.  **Single Source of Income**: All income depends on a job; once there is no job, the source of income is lost. It's easy to be tied to the main job, lacking options and a sense of security.
2.  **The Drain of Constant "Proving Value"**: In job hunting and work, one needs to constantly prove one's value to the company. Sometimes one has to do things that one doesn't really want to do, or that aren't necessarily important. My ideal state is: value should be verified by users or myself, not just being accountable to a company.
3.  **Internal Company Permissions Limit Learning and Growth**: Authority for many important things is held by supervisors or the organization. Before wanting to do something, one must first get permission. If the company doesn't give the opportunity, one cannot accumulate relevant experience. If looking for a job in the future requires this experience, one might be in a dilemma due to lack of past experience.

In summary, I feel the value of a side hustle is:

> To allow oneself to possess both freedom and dominance, rather than betting all development and income on a company.

As for what the side hustle will be... allow me to keep it a secret for now 😂

## The AI Wave

In January, quite a few AI tools appeared again, such as Claude Cowork, Typeless, Clawdbot, etc.

Usually, as soon as these tools come out, the community is filled with various hype reviews, making people feel a bit of FOMO.

But I don't know if it's because I've been through several "baptisms" before, now I seem to hold a wait-and-see attitude towards these tools. If it can survive for three months, has no security issues, and people continue to use it, then it's not too late for me to use it.

And based on past experience, for those truly solid tools, big tech companies will actually slowly verify similar functions, or even make them better to use.

I always have a feeling that **our own capabilities are actually the bottleneck of AI**.

Generating AI at this stage has a characteristic: "uncertainty." Even if the probability is low, sometimes there are still errors. So in a Production environment, human intervention is often still needed for final confirmation.

Under this premise, no matter how fast AI generates, it will eventually get stuck at human review, which is the pass of "time" and "ability."

Because human time is limited, AI will be stuck by us who are Reviewing.

Because human ability is limited, we might not understand whether the things written by AI are reliable.

Regarding this problem, some people have proposed different ideas, for example:

-   Pair with development processes like SDD, BDD, etc., to avoid AI generating errors by clearly defining specifications, significantly reducing the burden of human Review.
-   100% control by AI. After AI generates, it is also deployed and monitored by AI. If there are errors, they are fixed immediately. Since humans are not involved, they won't be a bottleneck, and there's no need to worry about reliability.

I think both solutions are quite interesting, but I haven't researched them deeply myself XD.

Besides the two bottlenecks of "time" and "ability," energy and financial resources will also be a problem, after all, AI computing requires a huge amount of energy and money.

Seeing this, some people might think I am an "AI pessimist." Actually not really, I just feel there is no need to rush so much, and one shouldn't spend huge amounts of time chasing these new things.

In the community, everyone seems to be frantically hyping, but back in real life, the truly active users are actually the minority.

Yes, don't doubt it. Some software engineer friends I know are still only using ChatGPT-Like Web Apps up to now, and haven't even started using AI Agents.

Anyway, my own strategy is to learn slowly, focusing on solving problems. If I happen to find that AI can help, then try using it. If not, then look at it later. Instead of using AI for the sake of using AI.

On the contrary, **depth and breadth of knowledge** are what I feel will become increasingly important in the future, and what I want to spend time investing in mainly. The reasons are as follows:

-   **Judgment**: Having enough depth and breadth of knowledge allows one to judge whether the information given by AI is reliable.
-   **Imagination**: After all, for someone who doesn't even have a single idea, it's hard for AI to help, right?

However, recently I plan to study Claude Code properly, including concepts like Skill, Subagent, memory management, etc. After all, Claude Code has lasted for quite a while, and after trying it recently, it is indeed very good to use in work.

The next thing I want to research might be SDD, but I'll wait until Claude Code research reaches a milestone.

## Health

After starting work, I haven't done a proper health checkup. Just taking advantage of the company subsidy, I went to do it.

Actually, most items were within my expectations. Some I've had since childhood, some are very mild.

But one item surprised me quite a bit, which is the low SDNN index.

Checking online, SDNN is an indicator reflecting the function of the heart's autonomic nervous system. Lower values mean worse autonomic nervous system function, i.e., autonomic nervous system dysregulation. It warns that the body is facing high stress, chronic inflammation, excessive fatigue, aging, or obesity.

And thinking back carefully, I realized that in recent months I have indeed been more prone to palpitations, cold hands and feet, poor sleep quality, and even having diarrhea every day despite no stomach pain.

I'm only 26, and my exercise volume should be much higher than the average person, so it shouldn't be aging or obesity.

Could it be... too much stress?

But actually, I've never particularly felt I have any stress. I consider my work relatively relaxed, and there aren't many things to worry about in life.

However, reviewing carefully again, I found that I seem to be thinking about work or career matters all the time, and I have a strong sense of anxiety, very afraid of being eliminated, very afraid that I have no value.

But actually, I've always taken this anxiety for granted. I feel that a motivated person should constantly review where they are not good enough. Before achieving excellence, I should maintain high-intensity learning and cannot stop.

But this health check result made me rethink this matter. Perhaps I should find a way to improve this.

The methods I can think of currently are:

-   Try to deeper focus when working, and finish company matters during work hours as much as possible. If there are things I want to learn, try to arrange them in the gaps of work, or at most occupy a short period after work.
-   Holidays should focus on rest, go outdoors more. Don't stay locked in the room sitting in front of the computer even on holidays.

I don't know if it will be effective, anyway, try it first.
