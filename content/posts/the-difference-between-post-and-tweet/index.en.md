Currently, this blog is divided into two types of content: **Articles** and **Tweets**.

A few days ago, while I was writing, a question suddenly popped into my mind:

> Which category should this text actually go into?

So, I decided to write an article to clarify my thoughts.

## Flow and Stock

First, I want to cite Robin Sloan’s article, [Stock and Flow](https://snarkmarket.com/2010/4890/).

The author simply divides digital media into two forms of content:

- **Flow**
    - **Definition:** Refers to the feed on social media, such as Twitter, posts, or daily updates.
    - **Characteristics:** Ephemeral, fast, and focuses on the present. It is a mechanism to remind people "you still exist," with the purpose of maintaining a connection with the audience.

- **Stock**
    - **Definition:** Refers to content with enduring value, such as books, in-depth articles, carefully crafted videos, or projects.
    - **Characteristics:** Durable (stands the test of time). It remains valuable two months or even two years later. This is content people discover through search engines and serves as the foundation (Capital) for accumulating followers.

There is no superiority or inferiority between Flow and Stock; they each have their uses and should be complementary: maintaining activity through "Flow" while polishing high-quality "Stock" in the background.

- **Flow serves Stock:** Even if you create great work (Stock), without a daily Flow channel to promote it, you have to find your audience from scratch, which creates a huge opportunity cost.
- **Stock supports Flow:** Pure Flow often lacks depth. Without Stock as a core, creators easily become mere manufacturers of information noise.

> And the real magic trick is to put them both together. To keep the ball bouncing with your flow—to maintain that open channel of communication—while you work on some kick-ass stock in the background.

After reading this article, I had two takeaways:
- Stock and Flow are equally important. It seems that my current direction of updating both forms of content simultaneously is correct.
- "Time" is an important measuring factor; that is, will this creation remain valuable in the long run?

## Conclusion

After finishing this article, I realized it was much shorter than I imagined.

The reason is that I feel the content of this post has long-term value. The article I referenced was published in 2010, and here I am in 2025 thinking about the exact same issue, which should be enough to prove it stands the test of time haha.

In short, going forward, I will use "whether the content is worth long-term reference" to judge whether it belongs in "Articles" or "Tweets."
