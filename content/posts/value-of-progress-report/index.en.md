## What Problems Arise from Lack of Progress Reporting

Usually, if there is a lack of progress reporting, you will encounter the following three dilemmas:

![Problems of lacking progress reporting](/posts/value-of-progress-report/image1.png)

### Falling into the Cycle of "All Pain, No Gain"

Working hard and solving problems, but because it wasn't shown at the right time, performance is not seen.

Never think that if you work hard and make everything go smoothly, the boss will see it all.

When everything goes smoothly for a long time, the boss will only take it for granted, and won't think about the blood and sweat behind it.

### Loss of Opportunities and Credit Being Harvested

Despite having strong abilities and a good attitude, promotions or good opportunities are always taken by others; even the results of one's own hard work are claimed by other colleagues because information was not synchronized in time.

This situation often occurs when what you do is a small part of the whole project. Because it is a small part, you don't particularly want to report it.

And when the entire project is completed, usually the PM or technical lead is responsible for reporting on the project, and the boss/manager/colleagues from other departments won't see your contribution.

### Isolated and Helpless, Lacking Resources

When encountering difficulties, because no distress signal was sent through the reporting mechanism, no one reached out to help, and the boss couldn't see your needs.

What's worse is if the project is delayed because of this, and others need to help clean up the mess, affecting your reputation in the workplace.

## The Three Core Values of Progress Reporting

I wonder what everyone thinks the meaning of progress reporting is?

At first I thought: "Oh, well, the company wants me to speak, so I'll just say something briefly." I didn't think about why the company wants us to do progress reporting, and the value it can bring if done well.

Of course, briefly saying what you are doing is also a kind of progress reporting. But it wastes the benefits that could come later.

I believe that progress reporting is essentially an important tool for **"Managing Up"**, and its purpose lies not in formal updates, but in achieving the following three strategic goals:

![Benefits of progress reporting](/posts/value-of-progress-report/image2.png)

### Establishing Consensus and Cognitive Alignment

Progress reporting can eliminate information gaps.

For example: Is what is currently being done in line with the manager's expectations for this project? Is there any misunderstanding of the requirements? Is it on schedule?

Through reporting, both parties can reach a consensus on the current situation and requirements: the manager can quickly detect if something is wrong and make adjustments; and the employee can also avoid doing work in vain.

### As a "Display of Work Results"

This is a "defensive" strategy.

Through daily and frequent synchronization, record your inputs, problems encountered, and the solution process.

When the manager already knows the process, it is difficult for others to harvest your results, and your contribution will not be underestimated.

Conversely, if you are used to only speaking up when you have achieved big results, it is very likely that you will be slowly harvested by others during the process.

I believe everyone has silently thought while listening to a manager's report: "You talk big, but this project wouldn't have gone so smoothly without me."

I have also met kind-hearted people who would proactively give credit to others in meetings, for example: "This went so smoothly thanks to xxx's help."

Of course, you should be grateful when meeting such people, and if there is a chance, you can also become such a person yourself XD.

But unfortunately, there are not many such people, so it is best to rely on yourself to claim credit.

### As an "Opportunity to Get Assistance"

This is one of the most important functions of reporting —— "Avoiding Surprises".

No one likes the feeling of the unknown, especially the feeling of "my house is on fire, but I'm the last to know", which makes people feel extremely uncomfortable.

And when managers are afraid that employees are missing information, a situation of "micromanagement" arises.

So when a project is about to go wrong, reporting early and explaining the solutions already attempted allows the manager to be mentally prepared and intervene to coordinate resources.

This makes the manager a partner in solving problems, rather than a blamer who is notified of failure at the end.

Over time, the manager can also feel more at ease believing that the employee will tell him about risks, he doesn't need to do everything himself, and naturally will grant the employee more authority.

And the employee also gets more freedom and won't be managed on everything.

## The Three Levels of Progress Reporting

After reading the content above, I believe everyone should be able to realize that the ultimate goal of progress reporting is to create trust.

But in reality, to what extent do we need to do it? After all, wanting to get so many benefits from the start of progress reporting seems a bit unrealistic.

Is there anything we can focus on first as an indicator?

To answer this question, we can slightly divide progress reporting into three levels:

![Three levels of progress reporting](/posts/value-of-progress-report/image3.png)

### Creating a Sense of Security

As mentioned above, no one likes the feeling of "unknown". "Unknown" makes people fearful and insecure.

Managers usually have managers above them, and bosses have board members above them. Managers also need to report to these big shots, but he needs to worry all day whether there is any information he doesn't know, and whether he will be asked by these higher-ups and result in him not knowing.

So the primary task of progress is to provide enough information to let the manager grasp the current situation, especially the risk part, to avoid him feeling frustrated because he doesn't know anything when facing his superiors.

### Creating Consensus

Confirm through reporting that both parties have a consistent imagination of progress and direction, avoiding the tragedy of "I think it's 90% done, but the manager thinks it's still far off".

### Creating Value

This is the highest level, the purpose is "to be seen".

Showcase work results and contributions through reporting, avoid silent dedication being ignored, or credit being harvested.
