There are all kinds of people in this world. There are some I try to avoid working with, and one of them is **people with unfounded confidence**.

What are **people with unfounded confidence**? Simply put, they are people whose source of confidence is a mystery to me.

For normal people, confidence might come from past successes, affirmation from others, or professional knowledge in a certain field.

Unlike **confident people**, **people with unfounded confidence** have several common characteristics:

- They like to belittle other people's methods or views.
- They always think their way is the best solution (when you have a hammer in your hand, everything looks like a nail).
- They intentionally or unintentionally drop hints about how great they are.
- They enjoy lecturing others.
- They talk confidently about things they don't understand.
- (Engaging in keyboard wars online).

Not everyone who fits these characteristics is necessarily **a person with unfounded confidence**, but they are likely close. 😂

## Why do I avoid such people?

Actually, these people are not necessarily annoying all the time. The main reason I avoid them is: **Risk Management**.

I believe that life is an infinite game. The point is "how long can you stay in the game," not "winning or losing in the short term."

For example, engaging in illegal industries might seem like a way to make a lot of money in the short term, but once caught, the risk could be imprisonment, which is unsustainable in the long run.

In the workplace or partnerships, **people with unfounded confidence** are essentially engaging in a high-risk gamble, for the following reasons:

### 1. Ignoring potential "Hidden Costs"

Because these people believe their plan is the "best solution," they often overlook details and potential pitfalls during execution, and even frequently ignore individual differences.

When a person cannot see the risks, they naturally won't manage them.

In a project, this might manifest as choosing an extremely niche technology stack just because they like it, without considering the difficulty of future maintenance and manpower costs. Or directly forcing the collaboration style of another team onto the current team without considering product attributes and manpower allocation.

### 2. Inability to Course-Correct

To err is human. But the scariest thing in cooperation is not making mistakes, but **being unable to correct them**.

When normal people encounter setbacks, they reflect: "Did I not consider something thoroughly?"

But for people with unfounded confidence, their first reaction is often: "It must be that others didn't cooperate well" or "There is a problem with the environment."

Since they rarely listen to others' suggestions, this leads to the entire team missing the golden window to cut losses when a crisis occurs, watching the train derail helplessly.

### 3. Excessive Communication Costs

Communicating with such people often requires you to spend 80% of your energy managing their ego and self-esteem, and only 20% on dealing with the actual work.

To make them accept an objective fact, you need to go around in a big circle to avoid touching their inexplicable self-confidence. For long-term cooperation that values efficiency, this is a huge mental drain.

## Conclusion

In the workplace, we all know that "trust cures all ills." In fact, many team problems stem from a lack of trust in each other.

And trust, to put it plainly, is a **sense of predictability**.

I know where the boundaries of your abilities are, I know you will ask for help instead of holding on when you encounter problems, and I know you will assess risks properly instead of rushing blindly.

But those with unfounded confidence usually bring more shock than surprise.

Life is short. To go further and more steadily, I would rather choose to walk with those who "know what they don't know," who are humble and cautious.
