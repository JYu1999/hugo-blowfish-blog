Before we begin, I must clarify: this is not a traditional book review. I want to use this article to share my biggest realization and transformation in 2025.

Furthermore, there won't be a Table of Contents for this post, because I don't want you to skip around.

---

This year, work left me physically and mentally exhausted.

Specifically, a sense of depression and powerlessness clouded my mind. It felt like my hard work was just skipping stones—vanishing without receiving any recognition or return from anyone.

Not only that, but just when I had finally rekindled my hope, adjusted my mindset, and tried to improve—feeling like I was actually performing quite well—I was harshly denied once again.

This situation started affecting my physical health. I noticed symptoms appearing: my sleep quality plummeted, and my feelings of fatigue and burnout worsened significantly. It reached a point where I couldn't do anything properly, which in turn affected my daily life.

I felt this was no longer just ordinary stress; I was likely stepping with one foot into a mental health crisis.

So, I hurriedly booked a counseling session and looked for some books to read, hoping to solve this problem.

---

One day, I took leave and forced myself to go out, finding a coffee shop near my home.

While listening to Lo-fi music and smelling the aroma of coffee, I opened *The Why Are You Here Cafe* (also known as *The Cafe on the Edge of the World*).

I actually don't know why I chose this book that day. Perhaps a mysterious force knew what I needed most at that moment?

Honestly, the plot is quite simple, perhaps even a bit boring.

But the questions asked by the characters, and the protagonist's self-reflection, triggered deep thought.

The book revolves around three questions:
- Why are you here?
- Do you fear death?
- Are you fulfilled?

I believe readers seeing this might think: *These three questions look simple, what is there to think about?*

But if you follow the protagonist's train of thought and ponder deeply, you'll discover that the answers aren't as simple as you might think.

---

After closing the book, I began to think about several things, including: Why exactly do I work? What is the meaning of my work? What do I hope to gain from work?

I wrote in my notebook at the time:

> How did I let my life get like this? Every morning I wake up thinking about earning money, finding a better job, learning new skills, pursuing efficiency and growth, and delivering value.
>
> But, what is it that I actually want?
>
> Where is the finish line?

At that moment, I recalled a video I had watched that mentioned the concept of an "Ideal Life."

It refers to what we hope our life will look like at the very end, after all our ceaseless striving.

I tried to think about this, trying to paint a picture of my ideal life.

But honestly, I had no answer at that moment.

I tried looking up the lives of successful people, hoping to use them as a reference, but it didn't feel like what I wanted.

I was like a person swimming in the ocean—swimming desperately, yet not even knowing where the finish line was.

---

That evening, I went for a run. The night breeze carried a cool humidity, and my steps and breathing achieved a wonderful synchronization.

I completely zoned out, slowly inhaling and exhaling in the darkness, focusing entirely on my body. It felt as if I was the only person moving forward in the world.

Suddenly, in a fleeting moment, a thought flashed by, like an electric current shooting from my spine to the top of my head.

An indescribable energy, like an airflow, moved through my body and finally flowed to my brain.

When my brain received that energy, it seemed to become "clear."

The sensation was so strange I thought I was running until I fainted XD.

Even now, I don't know exactly what that feeling was. If I had to use a word to describe it, it might be a sense of "well-being" (or bliss).

In that moment, I suddenly realized: **Isn't my current life already my ideal life?**

Why do I say that?

My material desires aren't very high. I don't want to buy luxury goods, sports cars, famous watches, etc.

I don't pursue frequent international travel, business class flights, fine dining for every meal, or a life of debauchery.

The life I want is actually very simple: I want to be healthy, live with my friends and family, have a dwelling where I can rest properly, and have enough food and clothing.

When I listed this out and compared it with my current life...

It's exactly the same 😇.

Suddenly, I felt the peace of the ancient poem: "Picking chrysanthemums under the eastern fence, and leisurely seeing the southern mountain." XD

---

With this realization, I discovered: The things I was pursuing before didn't seem to be what I truly wanted.

What I was pursuing were societal expectations—expectations from bosses, parents, and teachers.

This society encourages everyone to earn more money and acquire higher status.

Brands use marketing and advertising to heighten my desires, making me want to spend money on things I don't actually want. Yet, the more I spend, the more I need to earn.

But, can having more money really buy me an ideal life?

It seems not, because I am already living my ideal life.

Of course, if my income continues to increase, I might have better material conditions.

But ultimately, regardless of whether my income and assets are several times what they are now, my life will look pretty much like this.

---

With this mindset, I found that things like my supervisor's expectations or how much money I earn suddenly didn't seem important anymore.

After shedding these external expectations, I felt quite happy—or perhaps I should say, I felt "peaceful."

To use Buddhist terminology, it felt like a sudden "enlightenment."

However, I don't think I've completely liberated myself from that state yet. Sometimes I still care about others' opinions, and unconsciously want to pursue more things.

The difference is, whenever I catch myself having expectations for anything, or trying to grasp something, I remind myself: "Let it be. Everything will change, everything will disappear, and then appear again."

Facing any person, thing, or situation, I only have three options: "Accept it," "Leave it," or "Change it." All I can do is choose one and do my best.

Control my efforts, but do not expect a specific result.

---

But following on the heels of that is the next question: **Then what is important to me? Since I am already living my ideal lifestyle, what is the meaning of my continued existence? What should I pursue?**

I also suddenly realized that I feel just "okay" about the profession of software engineering. It just so happens that I have this expertise, it pays well, and I don't despise doing it.

But is software engineering my heart's desire? Obviously not XD.

So where should my life go from here?

In *The Why Are You Here Cafe*, I read that the "meaning of life" actually takes a very, very long time to explore.

So I won't know immediately what my life's meaning is or where my life should go.

The only thing I can do is spend time learning new things and not stand still.

In *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant*, it is also mentioned that everyone has their own "Specific Knowledge" (Unique Advantage). These are things that are easy for you to do, but difficult for others. Or things you can do tirelessly, forgetting to eat or sleep, while others require great "discipline" to do.

Because when you are exercising your "Specific Knowledge," you are actually "playing," while others are "working." I can spend 16 hours a day playing, but no one can spend 16 hours a day working.

So in the realm of "Specific Knowledge," no one can compete with me.

However, finding that unique advantage also requires time and experimentation XD.

---

So looking at this whole situation now, I really have mixed feelings.

On one hand, my efforts in the first half of the year basically went down the drain.

But on the other hand, if everything had gone smoothly and I had gotten the raise as expected, I wouldn't have had the chance to stop and think about the meaning of my life, nor would I have encountered this book.

If that were the case, I would probably have continued to bustle about, constantly sacrificing my liver (health) to satisfy others' expectations of me in exchange for higher rewards.

I also realized that this is the first time in my life I've truly stopped to rethink what I actually want and rethink what happiness is.

I wonder, seeing this, how do you feel?

Where is your finish line?
