I don't remember exactly when it started, but I've always had a dream:

> I want to try living abroad.

During my student days, for various reasons, I couldn't make it happen. After entering the workforce, I got caught up in the daily grind, and this dream slowly faded from my memory.

That was until September of this year (2025), when my company went on an employee trip to Tokyo.

During those five days, I explored the city by train and slowly realized that I seemed to really like this place.

There were a few main reasons:

First, its **diversity**. Tokyo is a truly international city. You can see all kinds of shops with different styles on the streets. Whether it's food or shopping, you are constantly exposed to different facets of culture.

Second, the **transportation**. This left a deep impression on me. When I walked on the streets back in Taiwan, I realized just how orderly the streets in Japan are. Vehicles yield to pedestrians, and the sidewalks are incredibly walkable. Walking there just feels comfortable.

Third, the **weather**. Taiwan is still quite hot right now, but autumn in Japan is very cool and refreshing. I discovered that I really enjoy the feeling of having distinct four seasons.

On the flight back, a thought popped into my head:

> Since I like it here so much, why not just come and live here?

## Feasibility Assessment

After returning home, I spent some time thinking. If I really want to make "living in Japan" a reality, what model should I use?

After all, "living in Japan" can take many forms: working, studying, starting a business, digital nomadism, working holiday, etc.

To clarify my thinking, I listed my requirements:

1.  **Long-term residence of at least two years:** I feel that "living" somewhere should be a long-term commitment. If I have the mindset that I can leave whenever I want, I don't think that counts as truly living there. To stay long-term, I need an appropriate visa.
2.  **90% interaction with the local environment:** I don't want to go abroad only to end up interacting mostly with other foreigners. I want to immerse myself in the local people, events, and culture.
3.  **Stable cash flow:** I still hope to have the cash flow from a full-time job to support myself and my family.

So, the only path left seems to be finding a full-time job locally.

## Is Immigration Possible?

With a general direction in mind, I started researching work visas in Japan.

During my search, I accidentally discovered a visa called the **"Highly Skilled Professional" (HSP) Visa**, which Japan created specifically to attract talent.

If you have this visa, or meet specific conditions, you can apply for **Permanent Residency (PR)** within one year (or three years).

At this moment, I suddenly started thinking:

> Is it possible for me to immigrate?

Actually, due to various factors, I've always had the idea of immigrating abroad.

 However, the standards for immigration are usually quite high. Because of family reasons, I'm not considering countries that are too far from Taiwan.

After checking the relevant information, I found that the point calculation for the Highly Skilled Professional visa isn't actually that difficult. If I can use this job hunt in Japan to also attempt to get permanent residency, it seems like a feasible plan.

However, in the scoring criteria, there is a category where being **under 30 years old** grants higher points. Given my age, it seems I don't have much time left to attempt this.

And so, a major goal was set:

> **I want to find a job in Japan within a year and obtain Permanent Residency within three years.**

## But, Do I Really Want to Go?

The next question that followed was: this seems like a massive project that might not only involve myself, but also my family.

Furthermore, when it comes to living, I really love Taiwan. Although it's definitely not perfect, I feel it would be hard to find another place in the world that matches my needs this well (or maybe I'm just ignorant).

Is my desire to go worth spending so much effort?

Thinking about the worst-case scenario, the worst outcome would be that I spend nearly a year preparing, and in the end, no company that meets my requirements is willing to hire me.

Would that year of effort be wasted? I don't think so.

Actually, I think in Taiwan's software industry, unless it's a top-tier foreign company, high-pressure startup, or special industry, many companies can be mastered in just 1-2 years.

Such an environment can sometimes be too comfortable, causing people to lose motivation to grow, which is actually dangerous for long-term career development.

Thinking carefully back, I've only had slow growth in software technology for about the past 2 years.

So through this project, if I can start slowly picking up some technologies that top software companies are using, and even try to introduce them to my current company, I think it could be significantly beneficial to both me and my current company.
