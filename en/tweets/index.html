Pure Person

19 January 2026

I like pure people.

If given a choice, I would choose to work with people who are pure.

What does “pure” mean to me?

Internal and External Consistency: Congruence between Thoughts, Words, and Actions
#

Pure people usually don’t “perform” for others.

They might be social and understand social norms, but they don’t live their lives as a character.

Simple Motives
#

Pure people usually do things more because “I believe this is right” or “I truly want to do it,” rather than for transactional reasons, calculations, or to win people over.

A Childlike Heart
#

Pure people often give off a feeling of being “easy to read.”

Not because they lack sophistication, but because they don’t rely on hiding to gain a sense of security.

They don’t lack an understanding of worldly complexities; rather, after seeing through them, they still choose simplicity.

They may know the world has many grey areas, but they still choose to live in a “clear, frank, and kind” way.

Self-Alert: Don’t be an “Unfathomable Person”
#

This year, I’m reading the book “Introvert’s Guide to Social Success,” which mentions:

Why do people keep their distance from “unfathomable people”?

Because people naturally harbor negative preconceptions and stay alert towards things they don’t understand.

To me, “unfathomable people” and “un-pure” are quite similar.

The book also mentions:

People feel comfortable and at ease when they are with “familiar” or “easy-to-understand” individuals.

While it sounds like common sense, it served as a reminder for me.

In the past, I often kept many thoughts to myself, afraid to speak up. For example, being a super socially anxious person, I would sometimes force myself to pretend to be extroverted. But inevitably, “the truth would leak out” because that wasn’t the real me.

But later I found that instead of that, it’s better to directly express my thoughts, such as: “I don’t like crowded places, so that’s why I’m staying in the corner.”

This makes me feel much more relaxed because there’s no pressure of wearing a mask; and others can fully understand the motives behind my behavior, rather than seeing me as an “unfathomable person.”

The First Step to Replacing Yourself: Learning to Express

19 January 2026

This year, by coincidence, I heard from two outstanding partners who told me they are constantly thinking about “how to replace themselves.”

However, I later discovered that their execution methods were completely different, which sparked some thoughts.

One person’s direction was “increasing ability levels.” He believed that if he could learn more skills, he would be more competitive in the workplace and naturally avoid “being replaced.”

The other person’s direction was “liberating oneself from repetitive labor.” He believed in finding the patterns in things and finding ways to automate them by any means so that he could have the time and energy to learn new skills.

Over time, I found that the first person didn’t seem to be able to achieve “replacing himself” because he only focused on “learning more skills” without outsourcing the work, whether through AI or human assistance.

I’ve always felt that when everyone is working, the brain is running a “hidden algorithm that no one knows.”

The judgments made daily, the ways problems are handled, the priorities valued, the risks intuitively avoided — these are not “results,” but a set of rules hidden in the mind.

The problem is, most people can only state the goal: “I want this result.”

But they can’t describe the process: “How I got this result.”

Perhaps sometimes when delegating to others, they might be able to “divine” your algorithm through reverse engineering or past experience.

But we all know that the most troublesome part of an algorithm is never the normal flow, but the edge cases.

Often, results look the same simply because of good luck, the right scenario, or few variables.

Once an exception, a gray area, or a moment of trade-off is encountered, those who haven’t explained the logic clearly will cause the entire system to collapse.

If you cannot document the logic, edge cases, and style of this algorithm, over time your workload will only get heavier, and you will no longer have time to learn new skills.

And I think the best way is to actively write down your various decision logics and try to synthesize them into a set of “principles” in your daily life.

If you want AI or colleagues to share your workload, you must first become the translator of your own brain. Algorithms that cannot be documented cannot be scaled.

Writing yourself down might be the first step toward freedom.

When Blogging Meets AI: My Experiment and Reflections

5 January 2026

This article is 100% written by AI

The Beginning
#

懶得咀嚼的人生領悟

4 January 2026
生活

The tweet above about being “too lazy to chew” was actually written using Claude Code.

Here’s what happened: One evening while eating out, I had a sudden realization I wanted to record. But there was a problem—my blog is built with Hugo, which typically requires me to be at my computer with VSCode to publish. Unlike Twitter or Threads, where I can just pick up my phone and type a few words.

This limitation had been bothering me for a while. I’d considered building a backend system, but that felt too cumbersome.

Then it hit me: why not use Claude Code?

The Creative Workflow
#

The entire process was surprisingly smooth:

  1. Downloaded the Claude App on my phone and switched to Code mode
  2. Connected to my blog’s GitHub repository
  3. Described my idea in natural language: “I just had this realization… please help me write it as a tweet”
  4. Claude quickly generated both Chinese and English versions and automatically created a PR
  5. Reviewed the PR on my phone, and when I found something to change, I just told Claude
  6. Claude immediately revised and updated the PR
  7. Merged after confirming everything looked good

The entire process required only my phone, and I didn’t even have to write the article myself.

Problems Solved
#

This workflow solved my long-standing frustrations:

  • ✅ Can publish anytime, anywhere (no longer limited to having a computer)
  • ✅ No need to build a complex backend system
  • ✅ Don’t even have to write the article myself (AI generates based on my ideas)

For someone who wants to casually record thoughts like me, this was nearly perfect.

Issues Discovered
#

However, after actually using it, I discovered some problems:

1. AI Fabricates Content
#

Some details in the article weren’t what I originally said—the AI “filled in” these parts on its own. While they don’t affect the main point, they’re definitely not 100% my original words.

2. Writing Style Discrepancy
#

If I use this method long-term, the blog’s word choice and tone will differ from my own. While the concepts come from me, the expression is AI’s.

3. Model Limitations
#

Claude Code’s current model doesn’t support image generation. When I need a cover image for an article, I can’t ask Claude to handle it all at once.

This reduces workflow flexibility—if cover images could be generated too, I could truly handle everything from my phone.

Perhaps I’ll try other AI tools that support text-to-image generation (like Google Antigravity, which I’ve used before) to find a more complete solution. But for now, the current solution is good enough.

This reminded me of an article I recently read by Huli: AI and Ducks, Laziness and Truth, which made an important point:

“Not everything that AI can do for you should be delegated to AI.”

Now it looks like my publishing workflow could be entirely handed to AI. But is that really what I want?

My Decision
#

After this experiment, I’ve thought it through:

  • I’ll still write most articles myself

    • Because writing itself is practice in expression
    • The process of recording life matters too
    • I want to maintain my own writing style

  • Occasionally, I’ll let AI ghostwrite

    • When I’m out and have a sudden idea but am too lazy to write it out fully
    • When I’m really lazy and just want to quickly record a concept

For me, what’s most important about blogging is “conveying my thoughts” and “recording my life.” Whether it’s written by AI isn’t the most important thing—at least not right now.

But I also want to practice expression and practice articulating with words. So I won’t completely rely on AI.

Conclusion
#

This was an interesting experiment. Technology certainly brings convenience, but it also made me reconsider: what do I really want?

Perhaps the answer is: stay flexible. Let AI be a tool, not a replacement.

Sometimes write myself, sometimes let AI help. What matters is that these words ultimately come from my thoughts and life.

After all, things that can be done lazily, do them lazily. But things that shouldn’t be done lazily—well, maybe don’t be lazy about those 😊

Leaving Breadcrumbs

3 January 2026

Recently, whilst organizing my resume, I couldn’t help but feel emotional.

Because I hadn’t developed the habit of taking notes regularly in the past, I now have to sift through a massive amount of Tickets and Merge Requests to recall what I’ve done. Although I used AI to assist, the process was still mentally exhausting.

Coincidentally, I recently watched some touching videos where people used AI technology to transform old photos into dynamic images (recreating wedding scenes or the moment a child was born). This made me realize something: with AI technology advancing so rapidly today, what we should worry about is not that AI isn’t powerful enough, but whether we possess enough “material” for AI to analyze.

Just imagine, if we can only provide education, work experience, or fleeting thoughts from the present, the “me” analyzed by AI would probably be just a flat outline. But if we can provide long-term diaries, mental shifts when facing setbacks, and other deep data, AI will have a better chance to accurately interpret us and even provide valuable advice.

In addition to preparing material for AI, I also recalled a very sensible saying I once heard:

“The brain is for thinking, not for remembering.”

This reminds me of computer architecture: the brain is like a CPU, possessing powerful computing capabilities, but the cache space is quite limited. If the brain is stuffed with to-do lists and noise, it often cannot free up space for deep thinking.

Through the action of “writing it down,” we can effectively transfer information from RAM (Memory) to the hard drive (Storage), releasing computing resources for the brain to maintain room for clarity and thought.

Therefore, in recent months, I have started to intentionally leave “breadcrumbs,” not only to give my brain space to think but also hoping that my future self can re-discover my present self through AI.

My “digital breadcrumbs” include:

  • Diary: Recording daily TODO lists, thought processes, emotional fluctuations, and gratitude lists.

  • Work Log & Resume: Detailed records of daily work content, resolved issues, and noteworthy technical discussions.

  • Project Log: Focusing on recording the decision-making process, ideas, and progress when I execute projects.

  • Image Records: Actually, I didn’t like taking photos before. But after seeing the power of AI video generation, I couldn’t help but start fantasizing: if I accumulate enough media material, will I be able to immersively return to any moment in the past through technologies like VR in the future?

  • Blog: Yes, the article you are reading right now is also one of the breadcrumbs I left behind.

I am planning to ask AI to help me conduct a deep “life review” and share it after accumulating enough content.

However, the data is still accumulating, so I’ll hold off for now…😂

The Essence of the Workplace is Mutual Benefit

30 December 2025

This episode resonated with me a lot, so I’m taking some notes.

How to View Work
#

In the workplace, we’re not looking for Mr. Right, and we can’t find Mr. Right. There will inevitably be some things that don’t sit well with us.

But how the current boss and company are is their own issue. Don’t try to change “corporate culture.” Bryan compares corporate culture to “marinade”—individuals entering it will only be assimilated, and even the boss themselves may not be able to change the corporate culture. If you don’t agree with the culture, just leave.

We should focus our attention on ourselves, treat ourselves as an independent company, and engage in B2B cooperation with the current company, focusing on getting what we want—whether it’s skills, money, status, connections, reputation, etc.

We should constantly ask ourselves: “What do I want to become in one year, two years, five years…?” After figuring that out, work backwards to determine what we should be doing now.

Before joining a company, you can do two things:

  • Assume each job will only last two years: Two years is actually equivalent to the time it takes to get a master’s degree
  • Maintain a “goal list”: Write down everything you want to gain from this company in these two years

If all goals have been completed and there are no new goals, that’s the time to look for the next partner.

This is actually very similar to what I’ve been practicing recently. I also have a list that records my “long-term plans for the technical department and product planning.”

This list is simply divided into:

  • Product-related
    • Functional: Features I’m interested in implementing, features I think can create the most value for the company
    • Non-functional: Such as refactoring, optimization, architecture design, etc.
  • Development process-related: Such as optimizing development speed, avoiding technical debt accumulation, etc.

In my daily routine, besides regular work, I invest time in these topics.

When I find it too difficult to push forward in the company, or when I can’t do what I want to do for a long time, I might slowly consider leaving.

This doesn’t mean the company’s direction is wrong, but rather that I can’t get the skills I want.

Career Burnout
#

Additionally, Bryan also mentioned how he overcomes career burnout.

He mentioned that he records work tasks and gives them a happiness rating from -10 to +10.

Over time, you can observe what you actually like to do and recognize your talents and passions.

To eliminate burnout, you can consider two dimensions:

  • Vertical development: Go deeper (e.g., challenge yourself from building 10-story buildings to 30-story buildings).
  • Horizontal expansion: Serve more people or learn extended skills (e.g., engineers learning fire safety, architectural aesthetics).

However, Bryan also mentioned not to blindly change careers. He doesn’t recommend directly switching careers without a foundation (e.g., engineer to pastry chef), as this is usually just an escape from the current situation, with a very high failure rate.

The Art of Resignation
#

Ideal resignation should be because you see a better goal, not just because you dislike the current situation.

So recognizing what you want is still the most important thing.

Additionally, Bryan specifically mentioned not to stay because of a “salary increase to retain you.” If you stay only because the boss increased your pay, your value in the boss’s mind will become a bargaining chip that can be bought with money at any time, your chances of future promotion will be very low, and other colleagues might also look at you differently.

A healthy attitude is to thank the company cheerfully and insist on moving to the next stage of your career.

Conclusion
#

Thanks to Bryan and thanks to Boen for letting me hear such a great program.

Although it’s similar to my realizations this year, I couldn’t express it so systematically.

Hope this helps you who are reading this.

Convention Over Configuration

Let It Go

12 December 2025

After entering society, I’ve really encountered all kinds of troubles.

Just to give some examples: using criminal threats for civil matters (thankfully not prosecuted in the end), cars parked blocking entrances, bringing pets to pee at doorsteps, treating others’ balconies as trash bins… etc.

I used to care a lot about these things, but over the past year or two, I’ve gradually become more composed about them.

Recently, I read “The Almanack of Naval Ravikant: A Guide to Wealth and Happiness” and came across a passage that deeply resonated with me:

“You always have three options: change it, accept it, or leave it. The worst thing is to do nothing, hoping for change without taking action, knowing you can’t leave but unable to accept. Most of life’s suffering comes from this struggle, or from weariness with reality. The phrase I say to myself most often is: ‘Just accept it.’”

“What I mean by acceptance is being able to remain calm no matter the outcome, finding balance and center. Often, taking a step back gives you a clearer view of the whole picture. No one can always get what they want. Accept what has happened with equanimity—the sooner you accept reality, the faster you can adapt. Of course, accepting reality isn’t always easy.”

Many times, reality is harsh—“changing” others is too difficult, and the cost of “leaving” the environment is too high. After the process of elimination, we’re often left with only one option: “acceptance.”

But acceptance doesn’t mean surrender. Since objective facts can’t be changed, at least my subjective state of mind is mine to control. Since I have to accept it anyway, instead of being bitter and resentful, why not try to face it with a more open, even slightly humorous mindset?

After all, life isn’t about pursuing fairness and justice, but about seeking inner peace.

Maybe I'm Just Not a Morning Person

8 December 2025

In recent years, “waking up early” seems to have become synonymous with successful people.

Many KOLs depict the beauty of waking up early in their videos, and numerous books studying successful people point out that one of their common traits is rising early.

However, is waking up early really that good? Is it suitable for everyone?

I have tried waking up early many times in the past, but this year I finally discovered: I am really not suited for waking up early.

The Benefits of Waking Up Early
#

Whether in books or videos, when explaining the benefits of waking up early, the following points are invariably mentioned:

Least Disturbance in the Morning
#

Indeed, if it’s early enough, others haven’t even woken up yet. But relatively speaking, if it’s late enough at night, you won’t be disturbed either.

Moreover, not everyone can really go to bed very early.

For example, I really like Muay Thai, and most Muay Thai classes are in the evening. Even if I wanted to switch to the morning, I couldn’t.

So if I were to wake up early, I would have to give up my hobby, switch to morning exercise, and train myself to get up even earlier.

Morning is When Thinking is Clearest
#

When you just wake up, you haven’t been exposed to the various noises of life yet, so this is when your concentration is strongest.

And because you just woke up, you have good energy.

But for me, it’s not like that. Night is when my thinking is clearest.

I don’t know why, but normally, my energy curve climbs slowly from the moment I wake up, and then there’s a moment when it suddenly drops, which is when I should go to sleep.

It sounds incredible, but roughly 10 PM to 1 AM is my peak energy time.

In addition, the noise in life is not just from the current day; yesterday’s noise can also have an effect. Unless you can leave all troubles from before today behind as soon as you wake up, but the reality is that it’s impossible.

Morning Exercise Hormones…
#

Some people say that exercising in the morning releases hormones, so you will be energetic blah blah blah (please forgive me for never really understanding this part).

My personal experience after trying many times is: Zero.

My energy didn’t improve; instead, I felt even sleepier after exercising.

And my athletic performance wasn’t good either; I felt groggy while exercising.

I didn’t sleep better at night either; insomnia was still insomnia.

What Matters is What You Do When You Wake Up Early
#

Many people say that you need to set a goal for waking up early, set something to do, so that you don’t wake up early and achieve no growth at all.

But after my own experiments, starting work immediately after waking up is the most efficient.

Why? As mentioned earlier, I am groggy in the morning, so it is really hard to have the willpower to do difficult things.

Most of the time, I just scroll through my phone, do some housework, and then it’s time for work. So what’s the point of me waking up early?

Some people might say: “Then your willpower is not enough.”

I agree 100% with this, but for the same things, if I put them at night, I have the motivation to do them.

In addition, work has a forced wake-up effect for me because I can’t escape work; I just have to deal with things.

However, I can start with a simple review in the morning, or do simple tasks first, to slowly wake up my brain.

Conclusion
#

Actually, looking back at the periods when I grew the fastest, none of them were because of waking up early. On the contrary, when I was growing fast, I usually went to sleep at 1 or 2 AM.

So this year I decided to analyze it and decided not to follow this “early rising success philosophy” anymore, and accept my own physiology and habits.

I hope this article can provide some reference for you who are also trapped by the idea of waking up early 😂

Joining Wuthering Waves

Recently, I inexplicably wanted to find a mobile game to play, so I looked around.

I’m not a hardcore gamer; I play mostly for leisure.

My expectations for a mobile game are roughly:

  • Exquisite 3D modeling: I quite like watching beautiful characters running around on the map.
  • Not too brain-burning: I hope it’s action-oriented, without too many strategic elements.
  • Can play happily without spending too much: No explanation needed XD.

After clarifying my needs, I played “Genshin Impact” for a bit.

Actually, I had contact with it during university, and later quit and returned two or three times.

I think “Genshin Impact” is an excellent game, with diverse gameplay, and the story/music/art are all very good.

But “Genshin Impact” has a few pain points for me:

  1. Gacha pressure (spending pressure): A team has four characters. If the strength of all four characters needs to be online, it’s actually quite stressful. And there’s a chance to lose the 50/50 when pulling for signature weapons… QQ
  2. Repetitive modeling: “Genshin Impact” characters all use a few fixed underlying models, and then different skins are put on the outer layer. I feel a bit fatigued looking at them after a while, feeling that every character looks about the same QQ.
  3. Elemental reactions: This is the core combat mechanism of “Genshin Impact”, but I find it so complicated XD.

The deepest impression is that I spent a lot of money before to pull for Raiden Shogun and Furina’s constellations and signature weapons, but after pulling them and fighting bosses, I found it seemed to make no difference…

Are there other options?
#

Due to previous experiences, although I returned to “Genshin Impact”, I knew I would probably quit again soon.

So I started thinking if there were other options.

In between, I also downloaded the recently popular “Chaos Dream” (卡厄斯夢境) to experience it. The gameplay was quite good, but unfortunately, I don’t like card games XD.

Looking around, apart from the few games released by HoYoverse, “Wuthering Waves” had the highest acclaim. Then I saw a new character “Chisaki” (千咲) released recently, which I thought was quite nice.

Starting Wuthering Waves
#

My first impression of “Wuthering Waves” was extremely poor, because I briefly joined when it first launched.

But that tutorial segment was really… I didn’t know what it was doing. I deleted the game directly afterwards.

But after two years, I found a few things:

  1. Combat impact is great: For a mobile game, I think it’s not easy to achieve this.
  2. Many thoughtful little designs: For example, you can automatically teleport to the teleport point closest to the target, automatically fill in materials when upgrade points are insufficient… etc.
  3. Music, art, and animation significantly improved: Compared to the launch, the character ultimate animations are now super gorgeous. Some of the music really gives me goosebumps.
  4. There is a Mac version!!!: I don’t have a PC or game console myself, so being able to play on a Mac is just too enjoyable.
  5. Lower spending pressure than “Genshin Impact”: Includes only three characters in a team, lower pity count, weapon banner doesn’t lose 50/50, and constellation improvements are noticeable.

  7. Doesn’t have to be very brain-burning: The combat mechanism of Wuthering Waves is also quite complicated, but not wanting to study it doesn’t mean you’ll be doing chip damage.

#

In the early stages of playing, I set a limit: I won’t spend money until I pull a five-star.

Because if I can’t even pull one for free, then I think it’s too painful.

However, around the seventh day of playing, the first five-star came out. I decisively made the first top-up double + small monthly card XD.

But there are still some small shortcomings currently:

  1. Character materials gated by story: The materials for my current character require the story to be very far ahead, and I can’t fight for them in advance. It’s a bit painful 😭
  2. iPhone/iPad tends to overheat: I use iPhone 15 Pro and iPad Pro, and they get very hot after about half an hour QQ.

But overall I am satisfied ~ hope this game can accompany me for a while ~

Should I Use Other Blogging Platforms?

24 November 2025

After setting up this blog, a thought suddenly occurred to me:

Should I also cross-post my articles to other platforms? Like Medium, Vocus, etc.?

Or perhaps sync my “Tweets” to Threads or Twitter?

With this idea in mind, I excitedly went to research it.

However, halfway through the research, I felt a bit conflicted, so I decided to write an article to analyze it.

There was a previous post discussing why I built a blog:

Why This Blog Exists

19 November 2025·Updated: 20 November 2025
Blogging

In it, I mentioned three purposes for this blog:

  1. (Most importantly) Self-growth
  2. Helping others
  3. Accumulating influence

Let’s look at it from the perspective of “Accumulating Influence.”

I believe publishing to other platforms is helpful, as I can directly leverage their SEO.

Moreover, some platforms have revenue-sharing mechanisms or provide payment services that make it easy for readers to donate. For me, this is also an incentive.

Next is the “Helping Others” part. If the articles are seen by more people, there is a higher probability that someone will be inspired by my content (provided the content is good enough, of course).

Finally, there is “Self-Growth.” This is the part that makes me hesitate the most.

If I adopt the “I want it all” mode, I would have to post my “Tweets” to Threads and X (Twitter) separately.

And my “articles” would need to be posted to Vocus, Medium, and even other platforms.

If it were just a simple copy-paste job, it would be fine. But my blog uses Markdown format, so there will inevitably be things that need modification.

Furthermore, if I find typos later and want to adjust them, I might have to update every single platform, which would definitely be overwhelming.

Thinking a bit further:

  • Do I need to customize pages for other platforms? Like Vocus allows customizable Salons, and Medium has Publications.
  • How do I set up revenue sharing? Each platform has its own mechanism that requires setup, and while this is mostly a one-time thing, it still requires research.
  • Which platforms should I post to?

But, does investing so much time and effort really provide substantial help to my learning and growth?

In reality, I won’t write more seriously just because I’m posting to more platforms.

From a certain angle, perhaps there’s a chance to get good feedback on other communities or platforms. But my own observation is that it’s usually hard to get truly high-quality feedback on platforms. Arguments might be incomplete or superficial, failing to provide real help, or potentially even causing me to become addicted to the metrics.

So, in terms of self-growth, the benefit of this seems low.

Conclusion
#

After the analysis above, I decided to conduct a “low-cost experiment” first.

After all, the above is just the result of my mental simulation. In reality, I might actually learn more because of the variety of platforms. Or perhaps someone really wants to give me serious feedback that helps me.

But to avoid drifting off course later, I am setting some ground rules for myself:

  1. Post only, no maintenance: I will only proofread slightly when posting for the first time. If there are any updates or corrections later, I will only modify them on this blog.
  2. Drive traffic back to the blog: Every post will include a link to this blog.
  3. “Article” Platform Selection: The general principle is to choose platforms I am familiar with; I don’t want to spend extra time researching new ones 😂
    • Vocus: A platform I’ve posted on before; the traffic in Taiwan is quite good.
    • Medium: The first blog platform I encountered. Chinese content is a bit weaker there, but since I already have an account, I’ll give it a try.
    • Furuke: Supporting my company as an employee 🤣
    • LinkedIn: This is a bit special; I might post depending on the situation XD
  4. “Tweets” Platform Selection: The general principle is to use platforms I currently use; I don’t want to spend time creating new accounts 👀
    • Threads
    • X (Twitter)
  5. No reading or replying to comments: I will assume that people who really want to have a deep exchange with me will contact me directly via Email.
  6. Pure posting, no categorization or organization: Some platforms offer layout/formatting features, but I’m really too lazy to spend time researching them.

I’ll decide on the rest after observing the results later.

#

Should I translate articles into YouTube videos or Podcasts?

But… let’s leave that for later 😂

One step at a time, don’t get too greedy 🧑‍💻

Why I Bald Head?

22 November 2025

In the past few years, anyone who meets me for the first time always asks: “Why did you shave your head bald?”

So I’m writing this post to briefly organize my thoughts, haha. Next time someone asks, I’ll just send them the article (?).

Needs Analysis (Utility)
#

Perhaps we can start by thinking about the purposes of hair:

  1. For others to see, which can be further divided into two types:

    a. Professional Use: Some jobs might require giving clients a “professional” first impression, and sometimes a hairstyle can indeed be a bonus.

    b. Attracting the opposite/same sex.

  2. For myself to see: Being happy with how I look.

  3. Protecting the scalp/keeping warm.

First is professional use. My job basically involves working from home, and all communication with colleagues is voice-only.

Next is attracting the opposite/same sex. I think hair does have this effect. But I rarely go out, and when I do, I don’t want to interact too much with others, so I have no need to attract anyone.

The second is “for myself to see.” To be honest, I’d be quite happy to see myself with a cool hairstyle. But I think my bald head is also quite good-looking (says me!)

The third is protecting the scalp/keeping warm. Actually, I feel my scalp is healthier when it’s bald. As for keeping warm, the weather in Taiwan is so hot… If it’s really cold enough to need hair, wearing a hat is much faster.

Cost Analysis
#

Now we can analyze the costs of “keeping hair” versus “being bald.”

  • Time:
    • Haircuts: If you want a good-looking hairstyle, you usually have to go see a stylist every 3-4 weeks. But seeing a stylist means making an appointment, going out, waiting, going home… all of which take time. Conversely, a bald head just takes two minutes to deal with in the shower.
    • Maintenance: To keep my hairstyle looking good, I might have to wake up early to wash and style my hair. The time spent washing and blow-drying hair every night would also be longer. If I’m bald, I just towel-dry after a shower, and I can sleep in late in the morning! XD
  • Money:
    • Haircuts: Good stylists usually charge a premium. Conversely, for a bald head, I bought an electric razor for less than 1000 TWD that can last at least a year.

Someone might say, “Then why not just go to one of those budget quick-cut shops and get an easy-to-manage hairstyle? It’s fast and cheap.”

Actually, I experimented with this in college. The hairstyles that suit me usually require more “fuss” (or “maintenance”). The results from quick-cut shops usually make me feel like I might as well just go bald.

Plus, I’m genuinely too lazy to go out, and I really dislike queuing…

Conclusion
#

I admit, I occasionally wish I had a cool, stylish hairdo.

But whenever I put the “cost” and “benefit” on the two ends of the scale, I still choose to go bald without hesitation.

…Okay, I admit I’m just lazy 😂

Anecdotes
#

Once, when I was participating in a Muay Thai competition, one of my opponents also happened to be bald. The host called it the “Bald Head Battle,” which I thought was hilarious XD.

Another time, when I was running the Fukuoka Marathon, someone suddenly patted my shoulder at a water station, pointed to his head (bald), then pointed to mine, and said a string of Japanese.

I generally knew what he was trying to say, but my Japanese is absolutely non-existent 😧 I could only laugh and tell him: “Sumimasen, Taiwan-jin de, Nihongo wakarimasen.” (Excuse me, I’m Taiwanese, and I don’t understand Japanese.)

He laughed with a look of realization after hearing me, perhaps wondering why I was so stunned for so long XD.

The Charm of Solo Travel?

17 September 2025

In recent years, on social media in Taiwan, I often see people emphasizing where they went for “solo travel”.

In self-introductions, some people even specifically separate “solo travel” from “non-solo travel,” listing which countries they visited alone and which they visited with others.

There are even groups of friends who go abroad together, agree to have a free afternoon, and count that afternoon as “solo travel”.

This situation puzzles me. For me, travel is travel. Whether it’s sharing joy with a group or enjoying freedom alone, I enjoy it all. I’ve never thought about making a special distinction for travel.

If it’s for content creators, I can understand, as some people might need to find shops suitable for one person.

But for ordinary people, isn’t it a bit unnecessary?

I couldn’t help but wonder, what is the charm of the term “solo travel”? 🤔

So I asked Gemini about this question, and here is an excerpt:

Simply put, the charm of the term “solo travel” has gone beyond the act of “traveling alone” itself; it has been endowed with more meanings regarding personal growth, independence, and attitude towards life.

We can understand its charm from the following aspects:

  1. A Symbol of Self-Exploration and Growth

In modern society with its fast pace and high pressure, we rarely have time to truly be alone and talk to ourselves. “Solo travel” creates a perfect opportunity, forcing us to break away from familiar social circles and comfort zones.

  • Ability to solve problems independently: From planning the itinerary and booking accommodation to dealing with unexpected situations, everything must be done independently. Every successful problem-solving experience accumulates into a huge sense of accomplishment and self-confidence, which is an experience difficult to obtain in group travel.

  • Deep introspection: When there are no travel companions to chat and distract you, you will have more time to think. What you see and hear on the journey is more likely to trigger reflection on your life and values. Many people feel that their future direction is clearer after solo travel.

  1. The Ultimate Experience of “Complete Freedom”

Group travel, even with close friends, inevitably requires coordination and compromise. Who wants to go to the museum today, what to eat for lunch, how long to shop… these are all sweet burdens.

  • Freedom without compromise: Solo travel means 100% autonomy. You can sleep until you wake up naturally, or get up at 4 am to shoot the sunrise. You can spend the whole afternoon reading in a cafe, or jump on a train to an unheard-of small town on a whim. This feeling of “living completely for yourself” is a luxury in itself.

  • Courage to embrace uncertainty: Traveling alone makes it easier to encounter unexpected people and things. Because you are alone, your mindset will be more open, and you will be more willing to communicate with locals and try new things. This unknown and surprise is one of the most fascinating parts of solo travel.

  1. “Identity Labels” on Social Media

This also explains why everyone on social media emphasizes “solo travel”. In the era of personal branding, what we do shapes how others see us.

  • Image of bravery and independence: Saying that you are “solo traveling” or “like solo traveling” is like telling others: “I am an independent, opinionated person who is not afraid of being alone and dares to challenge.” This is a very positive social label.

  • Finding a sense of belonging in the community: Through tags like #SoloTravel, you can find like-minded people with similar experiences and values, forming a sense of belonging in an online community. Sharing solo travel tips and experiences is also a social behavior in itself.

I’m not sure if others find this analysis reasonable. I feel that the first two points are more like analyzing the differences of solo travel, while I think the third point, “Identity Labels,” answers my doubts.

However, if that’s really the case, I find it a bit of a pity.

There is a saying in The Analects:

At first, my way with men was to hear their words and give them credit for their conduct. Now my way is to hear their words and look at their conduct.

The general meaning is: In the beginning, when dealing with people, I would listen to what they said and trust their actions; now, when dealing with people, I listen to what they said and also observe their actual actions.

For example, someone might frantically praise how good cryptocurrency is and keep telling others to invest, but they haven’t bought a single coin themselves. This is a typical case of “saying no with their mouth, but their body is honest” (inconsistency).

Similarly, if someone says they can enjoy solitude but needs to deliberately put a label on themselves on social media… can that still be considered enjoying solitude?

I feel that this “I am independent, but I want you to know I am independent” actually reflects some kind of insecurity deep inside.

True strength is usually quiet and doesn’t need to be proven through broadcasting.

Deliberately labeling it as “solo travel” sometimes seems like a defense mechanism: “Look, I’m doing very well on my own!”

It’s like someone constantly emphasizing that they “don’t care about other people’s opinions,” but the very act of emphasizing it usually means they actually care a lot.
