After reading the case in the previous article, we can probably gather that progress reporting is an important tool.

But I am used to carefully considering necessity, risks, costs, and benefits before doing anything.

After all, time is precious and should be spent on truly important things.

So this article mainly wants to explore:

  • The push factor of not reporting progress: The pain points of not reporting progress
  • The pull factor of progress reporting: The benefits of progress reporting

What Problems Arise from Lack of Progress Reporting
#

Usually, if there is a lack of progress reporting, you will encounter the following three dilemmas:

Falling into the Cycle of “Only Toil, No Credit”
#

Working hard and solving problems, but because it wasn’t shown at the right time, performance is not seen.

Never think that if you work hard and make everything go smoothly, the supervisor will see it all.

Just like I said in the previous article, if you only report results, the supervisor will only see the results, and won’t know how much bitterness lies behind this result.

Loss of Opportunities and Credit Being Harvested
#

Despite having strong abilities and a good attitude, promotions or good opportunities are always taken by others; even the results of one’s own hard work are claimed by other colleagues to invite credit because information was not synchronized in time.

This situation often occurs when what you do is a small part of the whole project. Because it is a small part, you don’t particularly want to report it.

And when the entire project is completed, usually the PM or technical lead is responsible for reporting on the project, and the boss/manager/colleagues from other departments won’t see your contribution.

Isolated and Helpless, Lacking Resources
#

When encountering difficulties, because no distress signal was sent through the reporting mechanism, no one reached out to help, and the boss couldn’t see your needs.

What’s worse is if the project is delayed because of this, and others need to help clean up the mess, affecting your reputation in the workplace.

The Three Core Values of Progress Reporting
#

I wonder what everyone thinks the meaning of progress reporting is?

At first I thought: “Oh, well, the company wants me to speak, so I’ll just say something briefly.”

I didn’t think about why the company wants us to do progress reporting, and the value it can bring if done well.

Of course, briefly saying what you are doing is also a kind of progress reporting. But it wastes the benefits that could come later.

I believe that a more ideal way is to treat progress reporting as an important tool for “Managing Up”, and its purpose lies not only in formal updates, but in achieving the following three strategic goals:

Establishing Consensus and Cognitive Alignment
#

Progress reporting can eliminate information gaps.

For example: Is what is currently being done in line with the supervisor’s expectations for this project? Is there any misunderstanding of the requirements? Is it on schedule?

Through reporting, both parties can reach a consensus on the current situation and requirements: the supervisor can quickly detect if something is wrong and make adjustments; and the employee can also avoid doing work in vain.

I have a painful experience with this.

During the execution of a project, I spent a lot of time implementing a complex algorithm.

I spent several days and finally implemented it. After demoing it to the PM, he said: “Oh, actually it doesn’t need to be this complex.”

I could only tearfully revert several days of hard work…

As a “Display of Work Results”
#

This is a “defensive” strategy.

Through daily and frequent synchronization, record your inputs, problems encountered, and the solution process.

When the supervisor already knows the process, it is difficult for others to harvest your results, and your contribution will not be underestimated.

Conversely, if you are used to only speaking up when you have achieved big results, it is very likely that you will be slowly harvested by others during the process.

I believe everyone has silently thought while listening to a supervisor’s report: “You talk big, but this project wouldn’t have gone so smoothly without me.”

I have also met kind-hearted people who would proactively give credit to others in meetings, for example: “This went so smoothly thanks to xxx’s help.”

Of course, you should be grateful when meeting such people, and if there is a chance, you can also become such a person yourself XD.

But unfortunately, there are not many such people, so it is best to rely on yourself to claim credit.

In addition, I believe that progress reporting essentially helps us build our resume content.

If you consistently report progress and document it, you will be able to easily answer questions about what problems you helped the company solve when looking for your next job.

As an “Opportunity to Get Assistance”
#

This is one of the most important functions of reporting —— “Avoiding Surprises”.

No one likes the feeling of the unknown, especially the feeling of “my house is on fire, but I’m the last to know”, which makes people feel extremely uncomfortable.

And when supervisors are afraid that employees have information leakage, a situation of “micromanagement” arises.

So when a project is about to go wrong, reporting early and explaining the solutions already attempted allows the supervisor to be mentally prepared and intervene to coordinate resources.

This makes the supervisor a partner in solving problems, rather than a blamer who is notified of failure at the end.

Over time, the supervisor can also feel more at ease believing that the employee will tell him about risks, he doesn’t need to do everything himself, and naturally will grant the employee more authority.

And the employee also gets more freedom and won’t be managed on everything.

The Three Levels of Progress Reporting
#

After reading the content above, I believe everyone should be able to realize that the ultimate goal of progress reporting is to create trust.

But in reality, to what extent do we need to do it? Is there anything we can focus on first as an indicator?

After all, wanting to get so many benefits from the start of progress reporting seems a bit unrealistic.

To answer this question, we can slightly divide progress reporting into three levels:

Creating a Sense of Security
#

As mentioned above, no one likes the feeling of “unknown”. “Unknown” makes people fearful and insecure.

Supervisors usually have supervisors above them, and bosses have board members above them. Supervisors also need to report to these big shots.

You can imagine that one of the supervisor’s demons is worrying that he can’t answer questions asked by these higher-ups.

So the primary task of progress is to provide enough information to let the supervisor grasp the current situation, especially the risk part, to avoid him feeling frustrated because he doesn’t know anything when facing his superiors.

Creating Consensus
#

Confirm through reporting that both parties have a consistent imagination of progress and direction, avoiding the tragedy of “I think it’s 90% done, but the supervisor thinks it’s still far off”.

Creating Value
#

This is the highest level, the purpose is “to be seen”.

Showcase work results and contributions through reporting, avoid silent dedication being ignored, or credit being harvested.

Conclusion
#

I believe that after reading this article, everyone can fully understand why “progress reporting” is so important.

Everyone might as well recall, in the past when you were doing work reporting, did you simply view it as a “routine duty”, or an “important tool”?

As for how to do progress reporting? When to report? What to pay attention to when reporting? We will leave that for future articles XD.
